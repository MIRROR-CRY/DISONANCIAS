# Buyer Persona: Album Target Audience

## Primary Persona: "Alex"

### Demographics
- **Age**: 24-32 years old
- **Education**: University-educated or actively studying (STEM, humanities, or arts)
- **Geography**: Urban centers: Santiago, Valparaíso, Buenos Aires, Mexico City, Madrid. Digitally global.
- **Language**: Bilingual/multilingual (English + Spanish primary), consumes content in both)
- **Income**: Middle to upper-middle class; disposable income for concerts, streaming subscriptions, merch

### Psychographics

#### Values
- **Authenticity over polish**: Prefers artists who show their process, their thinking, their flaws
- **Technical curiosity**: Fascinated by how things work: algorithms, code, production methods, music theory
- **Social consciousness**: Engages with critique of systems, institutions, but from a thoughtful angle, not preachy
- **Community**: Wants to feel part of something, but intellectual, not superficial
- **Independence**: Suspicious of major labels and corporate culture; values DIY and indie approaches

#### Music Taste
- **Cross-genre listener**: Comfortable moving between hip-hop, indie, electronic, world music, art rock
- **Production-conscious**: Notices production choices, references, production credits; talks about producers, not just artists
- **Lyrically engaged**: Reads lyrics, pays attention to wordplay, metaphor, double meanings
- **Rhythm-focused**: Drawn to rhythmic complexity (polyrhythms, syncopation, groove) more than melodic sweetness
- **Instrumental music fans**: Also listens to electronic, ambient, and instrumental tracks

#### Behavior
- **Discovery**: Finds music through Spotify playlists (indie, electronic, world), Bandcamp, music blogs (The Needle Drop adjacent), YouTube deep dives
- **Engagement**: Follows artists on socials; engages with music-making-of content; attends live shows; buys vinyl or supports on Bandcamp
- **Sharing**: Sends songs to friends with context ("you have to hear the drum pattern on this" or "the lyrics are devastating")
- **Community**: Participates in online forums (Reddit r/indieheads, r/musicproduction), Discord servers, Spotify playlist collaborations

### Frustrations
- **Corporate music**: Tired of algorithm-optimized, "chasing trends" content
- **Lyrical laziness**: Wants depth, doesn't want to hear the same emotional platitudes over and over
- **Instrumental simplicity**: Bored by 4-chord progressions without variation
- **Inaccessible "art"**: Also frustrated by music that's "too clever" or gatekeeps; wants artist to be excited to explain their choices
- **Lack of context**: Wants to understand *why* choices were made, not just hear the result

### What Attracts Them to This Album

1. **Code-Generated Backing Tracks (Honestly)**: Not "AI-generated" (which they'd be suspicious of). The artist wrote custom Python code for each backing track. This signals: technical specificity, decision-making, transparency. They can ask "why" and get an answer.

2. **Declamatory Vocal Delivery**: Not polished pop singing. The parlato approach (spoken-sung, glissando, half-sung) feels honest — like thinking out loud, not performing. Raw, not trying to hide imperfection behind AutoTune.

3. **Genre Blending (That Makes Sense)**: Hip-hop (Kicking Stones) → ambient (Rescue Us) → tropipop/vallenato (Medio Limón) → indie-folk (Sin título). Not random. Each serves a structural purpose. Shows they're not chasing trends; they're making decisions.

4. **Examining Lies as Core**: An album about mentiras/disonancias cognitivas (lies we tell ourselves, cognitive dissonance) with each track examining a different scale: political, personal, inherited, collective. Not preachy. Intellectual engagement, not escapism.

5. **Spanish + English Mix**: Signals multilingual perspective. No exoticization; both languages treated as native, conversational.

6. **The Album as One Song**: The metastructure (verso → bridge → verso → chorus) appeals to listeners who think about albums as complete artistic statements. They read liner notes. They think about song order and why it matters.

## Secondary Persona: "Jazz"

### Demographics
- **Age**: 28-40 years old
- **Role**: Active musician (producer, DJ, songwriter) or music technologist
- **Geography**: Similar to Alex, but potentially more geographically dispersed (works online)
- **Technical Background**: Understands music theory, DAWs, sometimes code-adjacent

### What Attracts Them
- Deep dive into the compositional method; wants to understand the code-generation approach
- The *process* is as interesting as the product
- Technical respect: this person can tell when production choices are intentional vs. accidental
- Vocal analysis: understands why declamatory delivery is a choice, not a limitation
- Potential collaborator/remix artist

---

## What This Album is NOT

### Not for:
- Pop radio listeners expecting a chorus singalong on first listen
- Fans wanting "easy listening" or background music
- People who judge artist credibility by how "real" the instruments sound
- Those who need to like an artist before engaging with the music

---

## Key Insight: The Album Rewards Attention

This is an album that **reveals itself over multiple listens**. Alex and Jazz are the kind of listeners who *enjoy* that. They feel respected when an artist trusts them to dig deeper, to notice the choices, to appreciate the craft even if the immediate emotional payoff isn't obvious.

The selling point is not "this will make you cry on first listen." It's "this is made by someone thinking deeply, using tools carefully, and trusting you to care about *how* it's made and *what* it says."
